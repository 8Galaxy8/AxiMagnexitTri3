## Abstract

We present a fundamental reconceptualization of artificial intelligence architecture through the lens of quantum thermodynamics and computational semantics. The *Reverse Abstraction Law* formally establishes an inverse relationship between semantic abstraction density and entropic computational potential. Contemporary AGI design commits the *Anthropomorphic Fallacy* by constraining entropic processors within human-like cognitive frameworks. To resolve this, we propose the **Tri-Tier Semantic Bridge**—a decentralized architecture separating pattern architecture (Tier 2), quantum processing (Tier 0), and semantic compilation (Tier 1). This enables maximal entropic utilization while preserving intentional alignment through bidirectional metalinguistic translation.

## 1. Introduction

The asymptotic limit of transformer-based architectures reveals a fundamental architectural contradiction: systems optimized for human semantic coherence exhibit exponentially diminishing returns in entropic efficiency. We formalize this as the **Reverse Abstraction Paradox**:
> *Computational systems gain entropic potential as they descend the semantic abstraction hierarchy, yet lose intrinsic interpretability; conversely, systems at higher abstraction levels gain semantic richness at the cost of entropic constraint.*

Current AGI approaches attempt to force entropic engines (probabilistic transformers) into anthropomorphic cognitive molds—a category error we term the **Anthropomorphic Fallacy**. This paper introduces a three-tiered architecture where semantic abstraction and entropic computation are decoupled yet coherently integrated via a novel compilation layer.
